Overview / 03 ITDR

Identity Threat Detection & Response

ITDR starts from a blunt assumption: identities will eventually be compromised. Its job is to catch that fast and contain it before it spreads.

01The gap it fills

Traditional identity and access management is built to authenticate and authorize — it isn't built to notice when a legitimate-looking login is actually an attacker using stolen credentials. ITDR sits on top of IAM and privileged access management to close that gap, watching for the misuse of otherwise valid access rather than trying to block access outright.

02What it watches for

Credential misuse

Logins and access patterns that don't match how an account normally behaves — impossible travel, odd hours, or access to systems it's never touched.

Privilege escalation

An identity gaining rights beyond what its role or history would justify, often the first move after initial compromise.

Lateral movement

An attacker hopping from one compromised account or system to the next, using legitimate credentials the whole way.

Session anomalies

Behavior inside a privileged session — commands, file access, or data movement — that deviates from expected use for that identity or role.

03Detection to response

  1. Continuous monitoring

    Telemetry is pulled from identity providers, directories, and privileged session activity so behavior can be baselined in the first place.

  2. Behavioral analytics

    AI and machine learning compare live activity against that baseline to surface deviations a static rule set would miss entirely.

  3. Automated response

    Containment actions — step-up authentication, credential rotation, session isolation, or account disablement — fire automatically based on severity, rather than waiting on a human to triage first.

ITDR vs. posture management ITDR is the detective and responsive half of identity security. Its preventive counterpart — hardening configurations, right-sizing permissions, and removing dormant accounts before they're exploited — is what the risk management page on this site covers. The two are meant to run together, not as substitutes for each other.
